In 2007, World of Warcraft had a problem. Players were earning gold faster than they could spend it. Prices on the auction house doubled in months. A simple healing potion that once cost 2 gold now cost 10. The economy was drowning in cash, and Blizzard needed a drain. This is the story of sinks and faucets — the invisible plumbing that keeps every game economy alive.

## What Are Faucets?

A faucet is any system that puts new currency into the economy. Every time a player earns gold that did not previously exist, a faucet is running. Common faucets include:

- **Quest rewards**: Complete a quest, receive 50 gold. That gold is created from nothing.
- **Monster drops**: Kill a goblin, loot 3 silver. Multiply this by millions of kills per day.
- **Daily login bonuses**: Log in, collect 100 coins. Simple, automatic, relentless.
- **Achievement rewards**: Reach level 30, earn 500 gold as a milestone bonus.

In FFXIV, the daily roulette system rewards players with gil for running dungeons. On a busy server with 20,000 active players, each earning 30,000 gil per day, the economy generates 600 million gil daily — from thin air.

> **Word Notes**
> - *faucet* /ˈfɔːsɪt/ — 水龙头；（经济学中）货币注入来源。"Quest rewards are the biggest faucet in most MMOs."
> - *relentless* /rɪˈlentləs/ — 不停的，持续不断的。"The relentless flow of gold eventually breaks the market."

## What Are Sinks?

A sink is the opposite. It removes currency from the economy permanently. The gold disappears — it does not transfer to another player. Common sinks include:

- **Repair costs**: Your armor breaks after a raid. Pay 200 gold to fix it. That gold is gone forever.
- **Crafting fees**: Forge a legendary sword. The NPC blacksmith charges 5,000 gold. Destroyed.
- **Auction house taxes**: Sell an item for 1,000 gold. The system takes a 5% cut — 50 gold vanishes.
- **Cosmetic shops**: Buy a fancy mount for 20,000 gold from an NPC vendor. Pure removal.
- **Fast travel fees**: Teleport across the map for 100 gold. Convenient, but costly over time.

The key distinction is this: trading between players moves money around. Sinks delete money entirely. A healthy economy needs both circulation and deletion.

> **Word Notes**
> - *sink* /sɪŋk/ — 水槽；（经济学中）货币消耗机制。"Repair costs are the oldest gold sink in MMO history."
> - *forge* /fɔːrdʒ/ — 锻造，打造。"Players forge weapons at the blacksmith using rare materials."

## The Balancing Act

When faucets run faster than sinks drain, inflation hits. Every player has more gold, so every item costs more. New players suffer the most — they cannot afford basic gear because veterans have inflated prices beyond reach.

When sinks drain faster than faucets flow, deflation strikes. Players hoard their gold. Trading slows down. The economy freezes.

World of Warcraft learned this lesson repeatedly. In the Warlords of Draenor expansion, garrison missions showered players with gold. Faucets were wide open, but sinks stayed small. The result? Prices on the auction house exploded. A WoW Token that cost 30,000 gold soon cost over 200,000.

Blizzard responded in Legion by adding expensive cosmetic mounts — one spider mount cost 2 million gold. They also removed many passive gold-generating missions. Tighten the faucets, widen the sinks. The inflation slowed.

> **Word Notes**
> - *inflation* /ɪnˈfleɪʃn/ — 通货膨胀。"Unchecked inflation makes the game frustrating for new players."
> - *hoard* /hɔːrd/ — 囤积，大量储存。"Veteran players hoard millions of gold across multiple characters."

## Designing the Balance

Smart designers track a single metric: the ratio of gold created to gold destroyed per day. In a stable economy, this ratio stays close to 1:1. Some practical strategies include:

- **Scaling sinks**: Repair costs increase with player level. Higher-level gear costs more to maintain.
- **Percentage-based taxes**: A 5% auction house fee removes more gold as prices rise, acting as an automatic inflation brake.
- **Aspirational sinks**: Luxury items like rare mounts or housing decorations give players a reason to spend voluntarily.
- **Seasonal events**: Limited-time vendors that sell exclusive cosmetics drain gold in bursts.

FFXIV uses housing as a powerful sink. A small house costs 3 million gil. A mansion costs 50 million. Players save for months, and when they finally buy, massive amounts of gil leave the economy at once.

## Why It Matters

A broken economy kills player motivation. If gold has no value, earning it feels pointless. If gold is too scarce, progress feels impossible. The designer's job is to keep the water level steady — not too high, not too low.

*Every coin a player earns must have a place to go, or the whole system floods.*
