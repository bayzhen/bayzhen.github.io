Most people discover demands by accident — a personal frustration, a random conversation, a shower epiphany. That is not a method. That is luck wearing a disguise. A trained demand scientist uses repeatable systems.

## Method 1: Reverse Deconstruction

Take a successful product and work backwards. Strip away the solution and expose the raw demand underneath. Then ask: where else does this same demand structure exist, unsolved? Uber solved "uncertain wait time for transportation." Where else do people suffer uncertain wait times? Hospital emergency rooms. Government services. Freelancer hiring. The demand pattern is portable — only the context changes. Reverse deconstruction turns one insight into a dozen opportunities.

> **Word Notes**
> - *deconstruction* /ˌdiːkənˈstrʌkʃən/ — 解构，拆解分析。"Deconstruction of the competitor's model revealed hidden assumptions."
> - *portable* /ˈpɔːrtəbl/ — 可移植的（此处指需求模式可迁移到其他领域）。

## Method 2: Friction Point Observation

Go to any place where humans interact with systems — airports, hospitals, supermarkets, government offices — and simply watch. Don't ask people what they want; watch what makes them sigh, hesitate, or give up. A mother struggling with a stroller at a subway entrance. An elderly person squinting at a medicine label. A worker copying data from one spreadsheet to another. Each observed friction is a raw demand waiting to be anatomized and scored.

> **Word Notes**
> - *squint* /skwɪnt/ — 眯眼看。"She squinted at the tiny text on the screen."
> - *stroller* /ˈstroʊlər/ — 婴儿推车。

## Method 3: Behavior-Speech Divergence

Listen to what people say, then watch what they actually do. The gap between the two is where hidden demands live. People say "I want to eat healthy" while ordering fried chicken. They say "I don't care about appearance" while spending hours choosing outfits. The divergence is not hypocrisy — it reveals unmet needs that people are ashamed to admit or unable to articulate. Products that serve the behavior rather than the stated preference consistently outperform.

> **Word Notes**
> - *divergence* /daɪˈvɜːrdʒəns/ — 分歧，偏差。"The divergence between policy and practice was alarming."
> - *hypocrisy* /hɪˈpɒkrəsi/ — 虚伪。"It's not hypocrisy — it's an unresolved internal conflict."

## Method 4: Cross-Domain Transfer

Borrow a proven solution from one industry and apply it to another. Assembly lines moved from factories to fast food (McDonald's). Subscription models moved from magazines to software to groceries. The demand in the new domain already exists — what's missing is the structural solution that another domain has already proven. Cross-domain transfer is the fastest method because both the demand and the solution pattern are already validated.

> **Word Notes**
> - *validated* /ˈvælɪdeɪtɪd/ — 经过验证的。"The model was validated across three different markets."

## Key Takeaways

- Four repeatable methods: reverse deconstruction, friction point observation, behavior-speech divergence, and cross-domain transfer
- None require genius or luck — only discipline and systematic practice
- Combine multiple methods on the same problem for the strongest results

*Inspiration is a visitor that comes without appointment. Method is an employee that shows up every morning.*
