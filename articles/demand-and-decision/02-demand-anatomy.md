A doctor doesn't look at a patient and say "you seem sick." They check pulse, temperature, blood pressure, reflexes — each metric tells a different part of the story. Demands deserve the same systematic examination.

## The Five Components

Every demand, no matter how complex, can be decomposed into five structural components. **Trigger condition**: what specific event or situation activates the need? Not "people want convenience" — that is too vague. Rather: "a working parent arrives home at 7 PM, exhausted, with two hungry children." The trigger must be concrete and observable.

**Hidden pain**: what is the real suffering beneath the surface complaint? The parent doesn't just want food delivered — they want to stop feeling guilty about not cooking. The hidden pain is almost never what people say out loud.

> **Word Notes**
> - *decompose* /ˌdiːkəmˈpoʊz/ — 分解。"We decomposed the problem into smaller, manageable parts."
> - *observable* /əbˈzɜːrvəbl/ — 可观察的，可察觉的。"The trigger must be observable, not assumed."

## Frequency, Alternatives, and Emotion

**Scene frequency**: how often does this trigger occur? A pain that happens once a year is a nuisance. A pain that happens daily is a market. **Alternative deficiency**: what solutions currently exist, and why do they fall short? If current alternatives are "good enough," your demand is weak. The best demands target situations where existing solutions are either absent, expensive, or humiliating to use.

**Emotional driver**: what emotion fuels the need? Fear, shame, ambition, loneliness, pride — emotion is the engine that converts a mild inconvenience into a burning demand. People don't pay for solutions to problems they feel neutral about. They pay to escape pain or chase desire.

> **Word Notes**
> - *nuisance* /ˈnjuːsəns/ — 令人讨厌的事物，小麻烦。"The leak was a nuisance but not an emergency."
> - *humiliating* /hjuːˈmɪlieɪtɪŋ/ — 令人屈辱的。"The return process was so humiliating that customers just kept the defective product."

## Applying the Model

Take ride-hailing as an example. Trigger: standing on a rainy street, arm raised, watching occupied taxis pass. Hidden pain: the helplessness of not knowing if or when a ride will come. Frequency: daily for urban commuters. Alternative deficiency: traditional taxis are unreliable and non-transparent. Emotional driver: the anxiety of losing control over your own schedule. When all five components score high, you are looking at a demand that will reshape an industry.

> **Word Notes**
> - *ride-hailing* — 网约车服务。"Ride-hailing apps transformed urban transportation."
> - *non-transparent* — 不透明的。"Pricing was non-transparent, leaving passengers feeling cheated."

## Key Takeaways

- Every demand has five components: trigger condition, hidden pain, scene frequency, alternative deficiency, and emotional driver
- Surface complaints mask deeper needs — the hidden pain is where the real value lives
- When all five components score high, the demand is structurally strong enough to build on

*Don't ask "Is there a need?" Ask "What triggers it, what really hurts, how often, what else have they tried, and what emotion is driving them?"*
