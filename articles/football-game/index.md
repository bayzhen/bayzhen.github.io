## Why This Series Exists

You're about to join a football game project, but there's a problem: you've never really followed football. You don't know what a "false nine" is, why offside exists, or how stamina systems work.

This series fixes that. Fast.

## What You'll Learn

This isn't a generic "intro to football" guide. It's written specifically for game developers who need to understand:

- **The domain** — football rules, positions, tactics (just enough to not sound clueless in meetings)
- **The systems** — how real games like FIFA and Football Manager translate football into code
- **The engineering** — physics, AI, networking, and architecture patterns that actually ship

## How to Read This

Each article is standalone. Jump to what you need:

**Part I — Football Fundamentals** (01–03)
The minimum football knowledge you need. Rules, positions, tactics. Skip if you already know the sport.

**Part II — Game Design** (04–07)
How football becomes a game. Genres, attributes, match mechanics, AI behavior. This is where it gets interesting.

**Part III — Technical Implementation** (08–10)
The engineering layer. Architecture, physics, UI/UX. Code examples included.

**Part IV — Multiplayer & Abilities** (12)
Network architecture and Gameplay Ability Systems for online football games.

**Part V — Reference** (11)
A glossary of football and game dev terms. Use it when you're confused.

## Real-World Examples

Throughout this series, I reference actual games:

- **FIFA / EA Sports FC** — the AAA simulation standard
- **PES / eFootball** — Konami's physics-focused approach
- **Football Manager** — the deepest simulation, all spreadsheets and AI
- **Rocket League** — yes, it's football with cars, and the physics are brilliant

## English + Chinese

Articles are written in English with Chinese translations for key terms. Why English? Because:

1. Most game dev documentation is in English
2. Your team might be international
3. It's good practice

If you're comfortable with CET-6 level English, you'll be fine.
