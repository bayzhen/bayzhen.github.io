The people who built Suno didn't make money by generating songs. They made money by building the tool that generates songs.

## Two Kinds of Value

In every AI wave, there are two groups. The first group uses AI tools to do their existing work faster. They save time, they save money, and they feel productive. But their output is capped by how many hours they work each day — AI or not, a human can only review so many things before exhaustion. The second group builds the tools, platforms, or bridges that others use. Their value compounds over time because the product keeps working while they sleep.

> **Word Notes**
> - *capped* — 被限制，有上限的。"Your daily output is capped by your available hours."
> - *compounds* /kəmˈpaʊndz/ — 复合增长，利滚利。"The value of a product compounds because it serves users continuously."
> - *exhaustion* /ɪɡˈzɔːstʃən/ — 精疲力竭。"Reviewing AI output for eight hours leads to mental exhaustion."

## Your Rare Combination

If you've spent years in game development and also understand AI — reinforcement learning, model training, inference pipelines — you sit at an intersection that very few people occupy. Pure AI researchers don't understand game engine architecture. Pure game developers don't understand how models work. You speak both languages. That crossover is where the most interesting problems live: AI-driven NPCs, procedural content generation, automated playtesting, and the engine integration layer we discussed in the previous article.

> **Word Notes**
> - *intersection* /ˌɪntərˈsekʃən/ — 交叉点，交集。"The intersection of game dev and AI is where rare opportunities live."
> - *procedural* /prəˈsiːdʒərəl/ — 程序化的，按规则自动生成的。"Procedural generation creates game content through algorithms rather than manual design."

## The Patience Play

You don't need to find the answer today. The AI landscape shifts every few months. What matters is that you keep building judgment — working with real engines, observing what AI handles well and what it fumbles, noting the gaps that no tool has filled. When the right opportunity appears, you'll recognize it because you've been paying attention. And you'll be able to act on it because you have both the game development depth and the AI literacy to execute.

Don't chase the wave. Position yourself where the wave is heading.

## Key Takeaways

- Tool builders capture more long-term value than tool users
- The combination of game dev expertise and AI knowledge is genuinely rare
- You don't need to commit to a direction immediately — build judgment first
- Stay observant at work: notice where AI helps, where it fails, and where the gaps are

*The best time to plant a tree was twenty years ago. The second best time is while you're paying attention to where the sunlight falls.*
